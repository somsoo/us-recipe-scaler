    🧮 How the scaling works

    • Scale factor: servings you want ÷ servings the recipe makes, or your multiplier, or your pan's area ÷ the recipe pan's area (round π × (diameter ÷ 2)², square side², rectangle width × length).
    • What is scaled: the amount at the start of each line, written as a whole number, decimal, fraction (3/4 or ¾), mixed number (1 1/2, 1-1/2 or 1½) or range (2-3 or 2 to 3). An amount joined with + or "plus", as in 1 cup plus 2 tbsp, is scaled too. Numbers later in a line, such as a can size, stay as written, and lines that don't start with an amount, like "Juice of 1 lemon", are kept unchanged.
    • Units it reads: cups (c), tablespoons (tbsp, T), teaspoons (tsp, t), fluid ounces, pints, quarts, gallons, ounces, pounds, grams, kilograms, milliliters and liters. Other words, like "large eggs", are treated as counts.
    • Rounding: U.S. amounts and counts go to the nearest ⅛ or ⅓, with ≈ marking a rounded amount; exact halves between two steps round up. Metric amounts show whole grams and milliliters (one decimal under 10) and kilograms and liters to two decimals.
    • Tidy spoons and cups: under ¼ cup becomes tablespoons and under 1 tablespoon becomes teaspoons; teaspoons move up only to a whole or half tablespoon, and tablespoons only to ¼, ⅓, ½, ⅔, ¾ or whole cups. Lines with a range or a "plus" keep their units.
    • Never scaled: a line that starts with a temperature (350°F) or a step number (1. or 1)) is kept as written.
    • Metric equivalents: U.S. volumes are converted through cups and weights through ounces, from the exact scaled amount before rounding.

    Unit sources, checked 2026-09-30: NIST Special Publication 811, Appendix B.8 (U.S. cup 236.5882 mL, tablespoon 14.78676 mL, teaspoon 4.928922 mL, U.S. fluid ounce 29.57353 mL, ounce (avoirdupois) 28.34952 g, pound 453.5924 g). NIST Office of Weights and Measures, Metric Kitchen cooking measurement equivalencies (1 tablespoon = ½ fluid ounce, 1 cup = 8 fluid ounces, 1 pint = 2 cups, 1 quart = 4 cups, 1 gallon = 16 cups, 1 pound = 16 ounces).

    ❓ Frequently asked questions

    How do I scale a recipe by servings?

    Enter how many servings the recipe makes and how many you want. Every amount is multiplied by the servings you want divided by the servings the recipe makes, so going from 4 to 6 servings multiplies each amount by 1.5 and ¾ cup becomes 1 ⅛ cups.

    How does pan-size scaling work?

    The tool compares pan areas. A round pan's area is π × (diameter ÷ 2)², a square pan's is side × side and a rectangular pan's is width × length. The new area divided by the old one is the scale factor, assuming both pans are the same depth. Going from a 9-inch round pan (about 63.6 square inches) to a 9 × 13-inch pan (117 square inches) multiplies the recipe by about 1.84.

    How are the fractions rounded?

    U.S. amounts are rounded to the nearest ⅛ or ⅓ (⅛, ¼, ⅓, ⅜, ½, ⅝, ⅔, ¾ and ⅞), the sizes of standard measuring cups and spoons, and a ≈ sign marks any amount that was rounded. An amount under 1/16 of its unit shows as less than ⅛. Metric amounts show whole grams and milliliters (one decimal under 10) and kilograms and liters to two decimals.

    Does it convert cups to tablespoons and teaspoons?

    With Tidy spoons and cups on, an amount under ¼ cup is shown in tablespoons and an amount under 1 tablespoon in teaspoons (1 cup = 16 tablespoons, 1 tablespoon = 3 teaspoons). Teaspoons move up to tablespoons only when that gives a whole or half tablespoon, and tablespoons move up to cups only when that gives ¼, ⅓, ½, ⅔, ¾ or whole cups, so 6 teaspoons become 2 tablespoons and 12 tablespoons become ¾ cup.

    Can it convert cups of flour to grams?

    No. Turning a volume such as cups into a weight such as grams depends on the ingredient's density, which is different for flour, sugar and butter. The tool converts only volume to volume (cups, spoons and fluid ounces to milliliters) and weight to weight (ounces and pounds to grams).

    ⚠️ Please note

    Scaling multiplies amounts; it can't account for cooking chemistry, pan depth or your oven. Large changes may need adjusting, especially in baking, and bake times can change with pan size, so check for doneness early. Numbers inside a line, like a can size, are not changed.
